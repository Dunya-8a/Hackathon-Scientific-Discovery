# Project-direction assessment: real work or rabbithole?

## Verdict (blunt)

**The machinery is real work and worth keeping. The synthetic findings are a rabbithole, and the "pin-the-world, sweep-σ" experiment is the rabbithole crystallizing — do not run it as a path to a better *finding*.** You have built a genuinely good autoresearch-paper-generation harness: provider-routed LLM, a Karpathy-style loop with a load-bearing correctness gate that actually catches reward hacks, literature grounding, persistent archiving, a calibrated cross-family judge, and an honest writer that traces every number to a log and refuses to overclaim. That infrastructure is the valuable artifact and it is near-complete. But the *content* the harness is now producing — metric_dependency and data_availability on toy Gaussian worlds — is restating textbook statistics (averaging N i.i.d. reads cuts variance ~1/N; an LCB/uncertainty-penalized estimator beats naive argmax exactly when selection noise dominates signal). Those are correct, but they were true before you ran them and they teach nothing about FoO specifically because there is no FoO in the loop — no DAG, no beam sampling, no LLM evaluator, just `mean ± Gaussian`. The σ-sweep would make a preordained curve (LCB advantage rises monotonically with σ, ≈0 at low σ — you already predicted the exact shape in the research note) slightly more rigorous. That is polishing the proof of a theorem you already stated.

## Evidence

**(a) The machinery — genuinely valuable, ~90% done.** Reading `agent_metric_dependency.py` and `agent_data_availability.py`: the autoresearch loop is disciplined (baseline + K=3, revert-writes-best, separate gate). The gates are *actually load-bearing*, not decorative — the metric_dep selection-quality≥0.80 floor blocks the constant-walk variance-0 hack, the data_avail acc@maxdata≥0.90 floor blocks the data-ignoring selector, and the coordination log shows attempts genuinely getting reverted for failing them (checker_fp attempt 2 rejected for degrading recall). The Goodhart work is the standout: you found via `audit_judge.py` that your own paper/code critic axes *anti-correlated* with real Round-3 reviewer scores (code r=−0.29), rewrote the prompts, and added a `JUDGE_*_MODEL` knob so the writer (Claude) and judge (GPT) can be different families. That is real ML-systems engineering — most people who build a generator↔judge loop never check the judge against ground truth at all. This is the artifact a future employer or collaborator would care about.

**(b) The synthetic findings — elaborate restatements of textbook statistics.** Look at what the loop actually optimizes. metric_dependency: `evaluate()` averages `reps` i.i.d. noisy reads, variance falls ~1/reps — the result (0.001169 → 0.000054, "~95%") is the standard error of the mean, rediscovered. data_availability: the kept move is an LCB selector `mean − c·std/√n`; it beats argmax precisely in noisy/low-baseline worlds and is null in clean ones. That is the *definition* of when bias-corrected/uncertainty-aware estimators help — there is no headroom when argmax already separates the arms. Crucially, **none of this is about FoO**. The "walks" are 12–20 scalars with fixed means; there is no option-DAG, no beam search, no consistency-checker, no LLM. So the generalizable content is "use the standard error and don't naively argmax noisy estimates," which is known. The synthesized claim in `foo-evaluation-signal-bound.md` ("FoO's selection is bounded by evaluation-signal quantity/reliability; you can buy it back with more measurements or a noise-aware estimator") is *true and well-written* but it is a statistics truism wearing an FoO costume.

**(c) The paper output — good craft, no audience.** I read `20260530-135854-*.md` end to end. The writing is genuinely clean: explicit scope ("synthetic walks under Gaussian per-walk noise in isolation; we make no claim of a general improvement"), every number traced to the experiment log, a real runnable appendix, honest reporting of failed attempts. As a demonstration that the *harness* produces non-fraudulent papers, it succeeds. As a research contribution it has no reader: the hackathon is over, the finding is textbook, and the retrieved "literature" is visibly broken — [L3] is NaI(Tl) scintillator crystals, [L4] is a hexagonal iridate, [L5] is elliptical galaxies. arXiv keyword search returned physics noise and the writer cited it. A human reading this paper learns the harness works, not anything about reasoning agents.

**Correction-of-corrections signal.** The data_availability finding went: clean null (committed, written up) → overturned by 2 seeds → now "conditional" → confound found (LLM picks the noise level) → proposed σ-sweep to fix the confound. Each step is honest and well-logged. But notice the *shape*: you are spending increasing effort to nail down a property of a toy world you designed, whose answer theory already gives you. The rising rigor is not buying rising insight.

## Goal analysis

The hackathon goal (beat a 5.91 reviewer baseline) is gone, and **no replacement goal has been named** — that absence is itself the core finding. Four plausible goals, assessed:

1. **Reusable agent infrastructure** — *strong fit, under-served by current activity.* The harness is the real asset, but recent work isn't improving the harness; it's feeding it more toy problems. If this is the goal, the substrate (Gaussian toys) is the bottleneck, not the loop.
2. **Skill-building / learning** — *good fit, but with sharply diminishing returns.* You have already learned the autoresearch loop, judge calibration, and the Goodhart trap. The 9th synthetic agent and the σ-sweep teach you nothing the 5th didn't.
3. **Genuinely novel research** — *poor fit as currently aimed.* Synthetic-on-synthetic with a theory-preordained answer cannot be novel. This goal is reachable *only* by changing the substrate (real LLM walks / real tasks), not by adding seeds.
4. **A portfolio piece / writeup about building autoresearch agents** — *strong, under-recognized fit.* "I built a self-correcting paper-generation agent, caught my own LLM judge anti-correlating with real reviewers, and fixed it" is a genuinely interesting story. The valuable output is a *meta* writeup about the engine, not the FoO micro-papers.

The mismatch: the goal best served by the asset (3 of the 4 above point at infra/meta) is being pursued via activity (more synthetic findings) that serves none of them well.

## Next-move recommendation (ranked)

**1. (Highest leverage) Stop generating synthetic findings; write the meta-piece about the engine.** The honest, interesting artifact here is the harness and what you learned building it — especially the judge-calibration story (critic axes anti-correlated with real reviewers → rewrite → cross-family knob). That is novel, true, and has an audience (anyone building LLM-as-judge or autoresearch loops). Pull `audit_judge.py` results, the Goodhart note, and the gate-design lessons into one `docs/research/` writeup or a blog post. ~Half a day, no further LLM spend, and it converts sunk effort into a real deliverable.

**2. (If you want the engine to produce real findings) Pivot the substrate, not the seed.** Point the *exact same loop* at something where the answer isn't preordained: let the optimized "walks" be real LLM-generated reasoning options on a real task with a real (noisy, LLM or rule-based) evaluator — i.e., put actual FoO back in the loop. Then "noise-aware selection helps" becomes an empirical claim about LLM reasoning, not about `random.gauss`. This is the only path to genuine novelty, and it reuses ~all the infrastructure. Larger (1–2 days) but it's the difference between a statistics demo and a research result.

**3. (Cheap, do regardless) Fix the broken literature retrieval.** Every paper cites scintillator-crystal and galaxy papers because arXiv keyword search returns garbage for these queries. The `[P2] direct arxiv-ID lookup` TODO already exists — wiring `2502.12929` to a direct `/abs/` fetch would put the actual FoO paper in the retrieved set instead of physics noise. ~30 min, materially improves every future paper, and it's a real harness improvement (goal 1) rather than more toy content.

**4. (Do NOT) The pin-the-world σ-sweep.** Skip it. You already wrote the predicted result in the research note ("LCB's advantage over argmax rises monotonically with σ and is ~0 at low σ"). Running it confirms a curve you derived from theory; the marginal insight is near zero. If you feel the conditional claim is under-supported, a *two-sentence* caveat in the existing note ("the cross-seed comparison conflates the draw with the LLM's free choice of noise magnitude; a controlled σ-sweep is omitted as the relationship is analytically determined") closes it honestly without spending the 30 min + tokens.

**Recommended:** do **3** now (cheap, real), then commit to **1** (the writeup) as the project's actual deliverable. Pursue **2** only if you specifically want to keep the engine producing research rather than wrapping the project up.

## Rabbithole warning signs (specific, from the repo)

- **Theory-preordained synthetic-on-synthetic.** Both newest agents optimize a `mean ± Gaussian` toy where the winning move (average reads / LCB) is the textbook answer. The σ-sweep's predicted shape is *already written down*. When you can predict the result before running it, the run is confirmation, not discovery.
- **Agent proliferation without a consumer.** Eight agents (`my_run_agent` a/b/c/fast, checker_fp, method_bias, metric_dependency, data_availability) and 12+ archived papers, but the hackathon — the only consumer — is gone. The two newest siblings are near-verbatim copies of the checker_fp template (same loop, same parsing, same compose pipeline; only the world and metric differ). "5/5 coverage of FoO's limitations" is a completeness urge, not a goal — nobody is reading the set.
- **Correction-of-corrections with rising effort, flat insight.** data_availability: null → overturned → conditional → confound-found → proposed sweep. Every step honest and logged, but each buys more rigor about a toy you designed, not more understanding of reasoning agents. The increasing precision is a tell.
- **Methodology/prompt polishing with diminishing returns.** The coordination log's recent entries are dominated by prompt hardening, structural-requirement tweaks, and re-audits — refining *how* the harness writes, long after the harness writes fine. This is the comfortable part of the work; it feels productive and is low-risk, which is exactly why it's a sink.
- **Goodhart risk — well-identified, not yet fully closed.** Credit where due: `generator-judge-loop-and-goodhart.md` names the trap (writer+critic+judge all Claude), and you added the cross-family knob and a calibration audit. But guards 2–4 (held-out anchor enforced in-loop, stop-on-cross-family-convergence, diversity preservation) are explicitly "NOT wired yet." If you build loop B (evolutionary selection against your own judge) without them, the engine will produce confident garbage that scores 9/10. Do not close that loop until those guards exist — and note that loop B is itself a candidate rabbithole if its only purpose is to optimize toy papers.
